HOW TO PLAY

The deck. 32 cards — J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7 in each suit. Only the top four carry points: J = 3, 9 = 2, A = 1, 10 = 1. Twenty-eight points in the pack, which is where the name comes from.

The call. Four cards are dealt to each player and bidding opens at 14. You are calling how many of those 28 points your pair will take. The rest of the pack is dealt, everyone gets one more chance to raise, and the high caller names trump by keeping a card from their hand face down.

The play. Follow the suit that was led if you can. The highest card of the led suit takes the trick — until somebody turns the trump up, after which any trump beats any plain card.

Calling for trump. The card the caller put down stays face down, and it is out of their hand — they play the hand a card short. Anybody who cannot follow the suit that was led may call for it to be lifted, and whoever asks does the lifting, so the caller never has to give themselves away. Once it is up the suit is public for the rest of the hand, the card goes back to the caller's hand, and whoever asked must play a trump to that trick if they hold one. Nobody is obliged to ask — a hand can be played right out with the trump still down, in which case it turns over on its own before the last trick.

Marriage. The King and Queen of trump in one hand, declared only once the trump is face up. Declared by the calling pair the call comes down by four; declared from across the table it goes up by four. One declaration per pair.

Winning the hand. If the calling pair takes at least what they called — after any marriage has moved it — they make it. Fall one point short and the other pair takes the round.

Single round. One deal settles it. Whoever the call goes to wins the game.

Multi round. Hands keep dealing and each one pays game points to one pair only — the callers if they made it, the other pair if they broke it. The bigger the call, the more it is worth: 14–19 pays 1 · 20–24 pays 2 · 25–28 pays 3. First pair to the match target takes the whole thing, so a match always ends on a call won or a call lost.
